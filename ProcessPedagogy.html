Process Pedagogy

"Making Matters"

Process-based pedagogy is proposed as the method that removes the stigma from failure while boosting the confidence of students who have deemed themselves “not good enough” as writers. It operates under the axiom that writing instruction should focus on the process of writing, rather than focusing solely on finished written products. But what such approaches neglect to define is the very factor that they highlight, which is the writing process itself. Nancy DeJoy’s essay in Chapter 8, “Making Matters,” attempts to answer this very question: “a process of what?” Her response is that writing is a process of “writing the self,” rather than creating a process of textual ethos (DeJoy, 2023, p. 103). Rather than “consuming and adapting” processes via following pre-established models, DeJoy instead adopts a pedagogy that encourages participation and contribution, letting students maintain a certain degree of individuality while also finding their place among a larger group whose relationships to one another could seem “random and disconnected.”. This is accomplished by building her course around students’ passions and interests, how their love for those interests affects the practices that they cultivate, and how those practices can be applied to other aspects of their lives (105). To put this method of pedagogy into practice, DeJoy proposes two writing assignments that utilize responding to nonfiction texts. The first assignment involves students reading short pieces of nonfiction involving individuals that apply their experiences in specific scenarios to other contexts. After reading, the students are instructed to choose an audience that they believe would benefit from the insights that they gained during reading, then to address that audience when explaining what they learned from the exercise. The second assignment involves selecting lyrics from a song of the students’ choice, which are then read together with the class – each student then shares any experiences or events that they associate with those song lyrics. Finally, students are asked to share what new insights they gained from their selected song through the following discussion questions: 1.) How did you come to know the words of the song so well? 2.) What did you know about the songs, their meanings, and why you live then that you didn't know before? 3.) How did you create a frame for that understanding?

This is by no means a complete list, and these questions are intended to act as guidelines, rather than a strict template to follow (107). What these assignments hope to accomplish is to get students to rethink their approaches to writing, paying less attention to genre conventions and instead analyzing their own thought processes so that they can identify and address a certain need among their audience, in a variety of methods rather than one standardized method. DeJoy closes by referencing to Richard Lloyd-Jones’s essay “Poesis,” in which he classifies writers as a loose group of “poets, primeval makers, enabling the culture to know itself and connect its people into a productive wholeness” (qtd. in DeJoy, 2023, p. 110). If the “process” of writing is truly to be defined as the process of “writing the self,” as DeJoy explains earlier in the chapter, then writing the self is the first step in truly connecting writers into Richard Lloyd Jones’s idea of a productive wholeness.
